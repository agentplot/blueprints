# sounding

Some work ends in an answer before it ends in a change: whether to take
a major release, how to settle what a meeting left open, whether an
approach holds once someone tries it. sounding works one such question.
It opens a worktree of the repository the question is about, starts
one session there with the question and its sources, and ends on a
conclusion the operator decides: accept, go on, or drop. A finding
beyond the question goes to millpond as a capture of its own. The work
an accepted conclusion proposes becomes items, or entries in crew's
plan, and sluice lands it.

## Why it exists

A question asked in the middle of other work costs that work its
thread. A session that stops to read a major release's notes, or to try
a prototype, leaves its own job half done, and what it learns lives
only in its own scrollback. A question worked well needs a place of its
own and a session that starts from everything already known about it.
It needs an end the operator can decide, and a way for what it found on
the side to reach the inbox.

crew's elaborations (crew ADR 0019) and Flywheel's (24, 25) give a
question that shape: one session, one place, one document the operator
decides. sounding holds the shape once, as a tool of its own, so a
person alone, millpond, crew and later Flywheel all work questions the
same way.

## What it promises

- One sounding is one question, worked in one worktree, one session
  per round, and each round ends on one conclusion.
- `/sound` opens a sounding while the session that asked goes on with
  its own work.
- Prototype code never lands from a sounding's branch. The work the
  operator accepts reaches main through an item's worktree or crew's
  plan, and through sluice.
- A session never stops without a conclusion, or a note saying where
  the next round would start.
- A round ends when its timebox runs out, on a conclusion of what the
  session knows.
- The operator decides every conclusion, and never decides the same
  one twice (6).
- A conclusion never becomes a signal. Only an offer comes back to the
  inbox, as a capture of its own (62).

## Where it stops

sounding lands no code, writes an inbox only through millpond, and
plans across no items. It never decides a conclusion: the operator
does. Ordering items across teams is crew's planner today and
Flywheel's later. millpond keeps the inbox, and sluice tests and lands
the work.

## The tools beside it

- **millpond** is the inbox. It sends a sounding an item that asks a
  question, keeps the accepted conclusion as the item's record, and
  takes each offer as a capture. Its book is at
  [../millpond/](../millpond/).
- **headwater** reports releases newer than a repository's pins. A key
  in its herdr plugin opens a sounding on a release that needs
  judgment. Its book is at [../headwater/](../headwater/).
- **eddy** lists recorded meetings. A decision a meeting left open
  becomes a question with the meeting's transcript as a source. Its
  book is at [../eddy/](../eddy/).
- **sluice** tests a change as merged and lands it, and never lands a
  sounding's branch. Its book is at [../sluice/](../sluice/).
- **crew** runs teams, plans and bolts. Its planner and design agent
  call `sounding open` as crew calls millpond.
- **Flywheel** is built later. Each of an intent's elaborations is a
  sounding.

## How to read this book

The chapters follow one sounding: the question and its sources, the
place it is worked in, and the three exits its session has. Then come
the stop check and the timebox that bound a round, and the operator's
decision on its conclusion. Last come the setups questions arrive from
and the surfaces the operator works a sounding through.

A bare number in parentheses cites Flywheel's statement,
`design/flywheel-next/requirements.md`, whose elaboration sounding
keeps the shape of. A rule id such as `code.1` cites crew's
`docs/architecture/`. sounding's own statement is not written yet;
[Open questions](open.md) says what it will hold.
