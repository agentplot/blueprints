Three decisions: does flywheel move out, what travels with it, and when

The flywheel is machinery blueprints happens to host. These three decide whether it stays a guest, what it takes if it leaves, and whether it leaves before or after the two end-to-end runs. They are one decision in three parts — answer them together and the whole handoff list re-sorts once instead of three times.

Headline: the cost that was supposed to decide this is gone, and so is the cost that was supposed to sequence it.

The development loop for a plugin under active edit is --plugin-dir + /reload-plugins — edit and reload, no publish cycle. And the namespace rename the research called "a blast radius" measures, when you actually count it, at eight lines across seven files, one of which is in one end-to-end script. Both blockers priced out at near zero. What is left to decide is not cost, it is where this thing belongs.

What "flywheel" is made of, and where a cut could fall

Two piles. The machinery is the thing that could ship — skills, profiles, schemas, the samples that teach them. The record is what the machinery produced and is still producing — the intent change, the book chapters, the two run scripts. Every option below is a different place to draw the line between them.

The evidence, and what it did to the argument

Seven facts. Four came out of the research doc; three I verified by hand in this session because the answer changed the shape of the options. Two of them contradict the research in a way that matters.

documented The dev loop survives the move.

claude --plugin-dir /path/to/flywheel loads a plugin straight from a directory — no marketplace, no install. /reload-plugins picks up edits in a live session. The loop is edit → reload, not edit → publish → reinstall.

verified here Skills are namespaced.

Built a throwaway plugin, loaded it with --plugin-dir, asked the session to name its own skills. It answered testplug:demo. So flywheel-inception becomes flywheel:inception the day it ships. Confirms the research.

verified here — corrects the research Agent launch names survive unchanged.

claude --agent agent-creator resolves to the installed plugin's plugin-dev:agent-creator — the bare name works, namespaced also works. So every herdr agent start … -- --agent flywheel-intent-conductor line in both end-to-end scripts, both skills, and the conduct skill does not change. The research assumed it would.

measured — corrects the research The rename is eight lines, not a blast radius.

Because only skill names move, the real diff is: 4 profile bodies (one line each), 2 SKILL.md frontmatter names, 1 cross-reference inside flywheel-inception, and 1 line in E2E-construction.md. E2E-design-loop.md: zero. The schemas name no skills at all. This is the fact that dissolves decision 3's stated argument.

verified here OpenSpec schemas travel better than skills do.

openspec schema which --all reports three sources: project, user (~/.local/share/openspec/schemas/), and package. You already have a user schema installed (dev.codecorral.intent@2026-03-11.0 — note the reverse-DNS-and-version naming convention). The record carries a shadows field, so a project copy shadows a user copy — the exact in-place edit loop we have today, preserved after the schemas move out. There is also openspec store for registering standalone OpenSpec repos machine-wide.

verified here A one-plugin repo with its own marketplace is a proven shape — you already run one.

plannotator@plannotator is a git-sourced marketplace whose marketplace.json declares exactly one plugin, and it is enabled in this repo's .claude/settings.json today. A flywheel@flywheel entry would be one more line in devenv.nix.

verified here — constrains D1 willdan-marketplace cannot host a plugin that lives elsewhere.

All 25 entries in its marketplace.json use "source": "./plugins/<name>". Its own commissioning gate enforces that: marketplace_audit.py resolves repo / source, fails when it "resolves to no directory", then sweeps plugins/ flagging any directory not listed as an orphan — and tests/test_marketplace_manifest.py asserts the exact ./plugins/<name> string. So "flywheel lives in its own repo and ships from willdan-marketplace" is not a free combination; it costs a change to the audit engine, or a copy-on-publish step. Shipping from flywheel's own marketplace costs neither.

What the evidence leaves you deciding.

Not "can we afford it" — you can. The live question is whether flywheel is a general method that happens to be used at Willdan or Willdan tooling that happens to be generic-looking. That is a judgement, not a measurement, and it is the only thing D1 actually turns on. Everything below prices the consequences of each answer honestly.

Decision 1 · the repo

Does flywheel move into its own repo?

Blueprints is the single home for Willdan design books. Flywheel is not a design book — it is a general loop for turning intent into built code, with two OpenSpec schemas, two skills, four agent profiles, and its own samples. Today it is a guest in the books repo, and being a guest is why decisions/blueprints-is-a-built-repo.md had to exist at all: an agent kept reading "we're in blueprints" as "this is not construction," twice, after reading the skill.

The four options, with their real costs

recommendedA · Its own repo, carrying its own marketplace
A sibling flywheel repo in the bare layout, holding skills/, agents/, schemas/, the two sample changes as fixtures, and a root .claude-plugin/marketplace.json declaring one plugin. Blueprints enables flywheel@flywheel in devenv.nix; day-to-day authoring uses --plugin-dir ../../flywheel/main. Why this one: it is the only option where the schemas and the sample changes have a natural home — they are not plugin components and cannot live under a plugins/<name>/ directory without the marketplace audit flagging them. It also makes flywheel's own construction ordinary: the conductor runs on blueprints main, the built repo is flywheel, exactly like rocs-kit. The special case that produced blueprints-is-a-built-repo.md stops being a special case for flywheel. Cost, stated plainly: one more repo to keep alive — devenv, CI, its own wt.toml gates, a history migration (git filter-repo or a clean-import). Call it one bolt's worth of work, not a weekend.
C · Move into willdan-marketplace
plugins/flywheel/ beside the other 25. The marketplace's own CLAUDE.md already calls itself "the outer loop — its plugins author and audit the design books," which is very nearly flywheel's self-description. Genuinely cheaper: nothing new to stand up. Manifest, commissioning book, audit gate, books toolchain, CI, devenv — all present and dogfooded. Cost: the two schemas and the two sample changes have nowhere to go inside a plugin directory, so they land at the marketplace repo root — at which point you have a flywheel repo with 25 unrelated plugins in it. And flywheel becomes permanently Willdan-scoped: a catalog entry, not a thing you could hand to someone.
B · Stay in blueprints, publish outward
Machinery stays where it is; the plugin is assembled into willdan-marketplace/plugins/flywheel/ at release time. Cost: a publish step that has to be written and then kept honest, and the source of truth and the shipped copy drift the moment anyone edits the wrong one — the openspec-construction family already shows this failure mode, where plugin.json says zmx and marketplace.json says cmux for the same plugin. And the guest problem — the reason blueprints-is-a-built-repo.md exists — is entirely unaddressed.
D · Stay put and drop the plugin ambition
Flywheel is blueprints' internal tooling forever. .claude/ stays the delivery mechanism; nothing is namespaced; other repos never get the loop. Cost: directly contradicts this intent's Destination ("the plugin is marketplace-ready") and the last two handoff tasks. Worth naming only so the option is on the table rather than assumed away — if the honest answer is "I only ever want this in blueprints," everything downstream gets much simpler and two handoff tasks retire.

Recommendation: A — its own repo, its own single-plugin marketplace.

The deciding argument is not cost, it is fit. Flywheel has four kinds of content — plugin components, OpenSpec schemas, sample changes, and a method — and only a repo holds all four. Both alternatives that avoid standing up a repo end up recreating one: C by putting flywheel's non-plugin content at the marketplace root, B by maintaining two copies of the same tree. The plannotator@plannotator shape is already installed and working on this machine, so the distribution path is proven, not theoretical.

The dispatch-to-cloud question rides on this. It is filed as "becomes an ADR in the flywheel repo once flywheel has one." Answer A and that task unblocks into a real destination; answer B, C, or D and it needs a new home named.

Sub-question — does the design book go with it?

There is precedent both ways. willdan-marketplace carries its own books/commissioning/, and blueprints' own CLAUDE.md says per-target commissioning books stay co-located with the repo they commission — so a non-blueprints repo carrying a book is established practice. Against that: aidlc-design is 23 chapters and 4342 lines, and the flywheel is only part of it. Phases, foundations, system design, agent workspaces, the worktrunk substrate, session management, commissioning, contracts, verifications — none of that is the flywheel.

recommendedBook stays whole
books/aidlc-design stays in blueprints, unchanged in scope. The flywheel repo carries a README and the plugin's own docs, and cites the book chapters the way every other book cites its sources. Why: the book is the AI-driven-development method for Willdan, of which the flywheel is one mechanism. Roughly four of its twenty-three chapters are loop chapters. And the three in-flight Writeback tasks target exactly those chapters — keeping the book put means the split does not stall the rewrites. Cost: the plugin ships without its design book in the same tree. Someone reading the flywheel repo has to follow a citation to find the design.
Whole book travels
books/aidlc-design/ moves to the flywheel repo entire. Cost: the new repo needs the entire books toolchain — preview.py, check-mermaid.mjs, the mdbook devenv, the Pages workflow, the landing-page generator, the wt.toml gates. Blueprints' landing page loses its method book. And nineteen chapters that have nothing to do with the flywheel emigrate with it.
Split the book
Loop chapters (conducting, authoring-capabilities, spec-driven-construction) become a new books/flywheel/ in the flywheel repo; the rest stays. Cost: the same toolchain bill as the option above, and now two books that cross-reference each other across a repo boundary — plus it lands mid-rewrite, since all three of those chapters are open Writeback tasks right now.

Decision 3 · the sequence

Does the split land before or after the two end-to-end runs?

This was framed as a rename question: ship as a plugin and every skill invocation is renamed, so the sequencing decides whether the E2E scripts get renamed once or twice. Measured, that argument does not hold. The rename touches one line of one script and zero lines of the other. Sequencing has to be decided on something real, and there is something real underneath it.

What the runs are actually for, from tasks.md:

  • "one intent through the design loop end to end … every command works as written, or the skill, the profile, and the instructions move"
  • "one released handoff through construction end to end — the bolt loop under real load"

So the runs' product is edits to the skills, the profiles, and the schema instructions. That is the pivot: the runs are going to churn exactly the files the split moves. Packaging correctness — do the skills load, do the profiles resolve, does ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT} point at the right place — is a checklist you run once after installing, not something an end-to-end run discovers.

recommendedAfter — runs first, then split and publish as one bolt
The dispatch rename lands now in blueprints as planned (it is already the bolt both runs are blocked on). The runs go. The hardening handoffs they generate land in blueprints. Then one bolt does the split, the namespace rename, and the publish together. Why: the runs churn the files the split moves, so moving them first means every run-driven fix lands in a repo whose gates, CI, and conventions are themselves brand new and unproven. Moving settled files is a mechanical git filter-repo; moving files that are still being edited is a merge problem. And the split-and-publish being one motion is what makes the namespace rename happen exactly once — which was the goal the "before" argument was reaching for. Cost: the runs validate the loop in its current packaging, not its shipped packaging. Mitigation is a named acceptance checklist on the split bolt: install flywheel@flywheel clean, launch all four profiles, invoke both skills by their namespaced names, resolve both schemas from the user source, confirm no absolute path survived. That is a checklist item, not a research task. Real risk: a decision made and not landed rots. If the runs slip, the split slips with them. Guard it by filing the split as a Handoff task now, blocked by the runs, so it is on the board rather than in someone's head.
Before — split, then run
The split bolt absorbs the dispatch rename, stands up the flywheel repo, and the runs then exercise the loop in its final home under its shipped names. Genuine benefit: the runs prove the shipping path, not just the practice. Nothing is proven twice, and the decision cannot rot. Cost: puts a repo creation — devenv, CI, wt.toml gates, history migration, marketplace manifest, and seven handoff tasks retargeted — directly in front of the two runs that are the intent's whole de-risking step. The runs are what tell you whether the loop works at all; the split is what tells you where it lives. Doing the second before the first spends the schedule on the less informative one.
Between — split now, publish after
Move the files to the flywheel repo immediately but keep loading them the old way (symlinks into .claude/), so nothing is namespaced yet; ship as a plugin only after the runs. Cost: two migrations to buy one thing that measured at eight lines. It also depends on .claude/skills/<name> resolving through a symlink, which is not verified and would need checking first. This option was worth constructing to see whether decoupling helped; it does not, because the rename it defers is not expensive enough to be worth deferring.

The honest summary of D3.

The stated reason to split early — avoid renaming twice — is worth about eight lines, and one of those lines is in an end-to-end script. The unstated reason to split early is better: momentum, and the decision not rotting. The reason to split late is that the runs exist to change the exact files the split moves, and you would rather move a proven tree than a moving one. If you weight momentum above that, "before" is a defensible answer and I would not argue hard against it — but I would then insist the split bolt also carries the dispatch rename, so the profiles are not renamed in blueprints and then moved.

What happens with your answers

Each answer you queue and send closes its decision. This session then writes one decision draft per closed decision into sessions/2026-08-06-flywheel-own-repo/, plus a report of what closed and what stayed open, and hands both to the intent conductor — which finalizes the records into decisions/, gives this surface a row in design.md, checks the task line, and appends the handoff and writeback tasks the decisions imply. Nothing here moves; the conductor catalogues it where it sits.

Annotations anywhere on the page count the same as a queued answer — a note on an option, a strike through a recommendation, or a correction to a row in the travel table. Two things on this page are worth contradicting if you disagree: the claim that the namespace rename is cheap, and the claim that the design book should stay put.

One thing this session did not verify and flags for the split bolt: whether /plugin marketplace add <local path> copies rather than symlinks, which a community issue reports breaks relative sources. It does not affect the --plugin-dir development loop, only the local test of the shipping path.
